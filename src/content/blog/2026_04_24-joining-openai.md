I started at OpenAI on April 14. It's been a few weeks. The thing that hits first is the energy of the group, the intensity, the pace.

My first real exposure to AI was in college, twelve or thirteen years ago. The moment that actually shaped my career came right after, at BMW, in their IT innovation co-op in 2014 and 2015. Nineteen-node Hadoop cluster, CNN image recognition for manufacturing quality, ML benchmarking across multiple frameworks. I was twenty-two and had no idea what I was doing. I knew this was the thing.

Every role since has been a step closer to it. ML for change-risk and self-healing automation at GE Digital. A forecasting platform covering tens of billions in revenue, with MLOps, NLP, and graph analytics underneath, at EY. Agentic AI, computer vision, and generative AI for healthcare and life sciences customers at AWS. Perspectv on the side, just to keep my hands on it.

Each step, the work got closer to the actual problem. Closer to the customer. Closer to the place where AI is the product. OpenAI is the biggest step yet, and it's at the center of where this is all being built. I'm finally with the team setting the pace, at the forefront of solving these needs for customers. This is, literally, my dream job.

Honestly, I still can't believe I work for this organization. I can't believe I'm in a role that's going to have this level of impact, at a time I think is going to be hugely consequential for AI, and more importantly for our economy and a ton of different industries. The fact that I get to take part in it is huge. Super proud. Super excited.

The bar is really high. The work is really important. It really matters, and that means a lot to me. My personal motto has always been: solve really hard problems with really smart people. That's not a slogan. It's why I'm here. This is exactly what I'm here for.

The pace is insane. I'm here for it.
